# Shopping Cart Review — Victoria Ashworth

**Reviewer:** Victoria Ashworth, Greenwich, CT
**Date:** March 10, 2026
**Device:** iPad Pro (latest model), also reviewed on desktop
**Session focus:** Shopping cart experience evaluation
**Cats:** Duchess (cream Persian, 7), Countess (silver Persian, 5), Lady Grey (blue Persian, 3)

---

## What I Was Trying to Do

I wanted to add several premium items to my cart — a SleepNest ThermoPod for Lady Grey, a PawTrack Smart Collar for Countess, and a ClawMate Auto Groomer for Duchess — then review my selections, explore gifting options (my friend Margaret's Bengal just turned two), select a premium shipping tier, and proceed to checkout with the confidence that my order would arrive beautifully presented.

## What Actually Happened

Where to begin.

The cart page greeted me with a clinical HTML table — serial numbers, tiny product thumbnails, and a layout that reminded me of an inventory management spreadsheet from 2008. No lifestyle imagery. No product details beyond a name and a price with the decimal places truncated (does this platform not believe in cents?). The "Order Summary" sidebar is a stack of plain text lines — Subtotal, Discount(5%), Shipping, Grand Total — with the visual sophistication of a gas station receipt.

There is no gift wrapping option. There is no premium shipping tier. There is no option to add a personal note. There is no "save for later." There is no concierge assistance. There is no estimated delivery window. The coupon code field says "Coupon Code" in placeholder text with the warmth and exclusivity of a clearance bin at a discount warehouse.

When the cart is empty, I'm shown a generic SVG outline of a shopping cart — the same icon every free website template has used since 2015 — and the words "Your cart is empty" in plain text. No personality. No suggestion. No "Based on your previous purchases" or "Curated for Persian owners." Just... emptiness, presented emptily.

## How This Made Me Feel

Insulted, frankly.

I spent twenty-eight years building brands that made people *feel something* when they interacted with them. I know the difference between a transaction and an experience. This cart is a transaction. It processes items. It calculates totals. It has a coupon field. That is the entirety of its ambition.

When I shop at Net-a-Porter, my cart feels like a dressing room with a personal stylist. When I order from Labbvenn, every touchpoint reminds me that I'm purchasing something crafted with intention. When I open a box from MiaCara, the unboxing itself is an event worthy of my cats' attention.

This cart feels like it was designed by someone who has never purchased anything over $50 and thinks "functional" is a compliment.

## What a Luxury-Tier Shopping Cart Would Look Like

1. **Product presentation with dignity.** Each item in the cart should display a beautiful product photograph — not a compressed thumbnail in a 96-pixel box. Show me the product in context. A SleepNest ThermoPod in a beautifully styled living room. A PawTrack Smart Collar on a gorgeous cat. I need to feel *good* about what I'm buying, not feel like I'm checking inventory at a warehouse.

2. **Gift services as a first-class feature.** A toggle for gift wrapping — with a preview of the wrapping options. A text field for a personalized note, rendered in an elegant typeface. Premium packaging selection — because when I send Margaret a birthday gift for her Bengal, it should arrive looking like it came from me, not from a fulfillment center.

3. **Tiered shipping with intention.** Standard, Priority, and White Glove. White Glove should include scheduled delivery, signature required, and premium protective packaging. For a $150 product, a $10 flat shipping charge with no options is neither premium nor reassuring. I want to know *when* it arrives and that it arrives *perfectly*.

4. **A "Save for Later" section.** Sometimes I'm browsing for Duchess but discover something perfect for Lady Grey. I want to set it aside elegantly — not lose it forever because I removed it from the cart.

5. **Personalized recommendations in the cart.** "Customers who purchased the SleepNest ThermoPod also loved the ChirpCam Window Mount." Make me want to add more. Suggest complementary items. Cross-sell with taste.

6. **Visual elevation.** Subtle animations when items are added or quantities change. Elegant typography — not the same default sans-serif used everywhere else on the site. A color palette that signals quality. The gradient button at the bottom ("Proceed To Checkout") is the only hint that someone considered aesthetics, and even that feels like it was borrowed from a Tailwind tutorial.

## The Relationship Impact

I am the customer every luxury brand wants. I spend $25,000 annually on my cats without blinking. When I find something exceptional, I tell my book club (twelve women, all with cats, all with discretionary income). I tell the board of the animal welfare foundation I sit on. I tell my neighbors in Greenwich. My endorsement has carried products from obscurity to must-haves.

But I will not endorse a brand that treats the most critical moment in the customer journey — the moment I commit to a purchase — as an afterthought. The cart is where you *earn* my money. It is where you demonstrate that you understand who I am and what I expect. A spreadsheet with a coupon field does not earn my money. It processes it. There is a difference, and it is the difference between a customer who spends $73 once and a customer who spends $25,000 a year for a decade.

## Overall Rating: 2 out of 10

The cart functions. It adds items, tallies prices, and has a checkout button. That is the floor, not the ceiling. For a platform selling products at $70–$200 price points — products that position themselves as "smart" and "premium" — the cart experience communicates nothing about quality, nothing about care, and nothing about the kind of relationship the brand aspires to have with its customers.

## Detailed Scoring

### Visual Quality — 2/10
The cart uses a standard HTML table layout with thin borders and alternating background colors that feel institutional rather than inviting. Product images are confined to 96-pixel squares. Prices are displayed without decimal places ($73 instead of $72.99), which, while perhaps intentional, reads as imprecise rather than elegant. The Order Summary panel uses the same gray backgrounds and thin borders as every SaaS dashboard I've ever seen. The single gradient button at the bottom is the only visual element that suggests anyone considered aesthetics.

### Product Presentation — 2/10
Products are listed as rows in a table. Serial Number. Thumbnail. Name (plain text, no description). Unit Price. Quantity (a raw HTML number input). Total. Remove (a trash can icon). This is an inventory manifest, not a shopping experience. There is no product description, no material information, no "why you'll love this." Nothing that reminds me why I chose this item or makes me excited about receiving it.

### Gift Options — 0/10
There are no gift options of any kind. No gift wrapping. No premium packaging. No personalized notes. No gift receipts. No option to ship to a different address with a message. I send cat products as gifts at least twice a month. This cart makes that impossible to do with any grace.

### Premium Services — 1/10
Shipping is a flat $10 with no options. No priority tier. No white glove delivery. No scheduled delivery windows. No signature required option. No insurance for high-value items. No expedited options. The coupon field — with its hardcoded "techconnect" code — is the extent of the cart's sophistication. A coupon code is not a premium service. It is a discount mechanic.

### Exclusivity Signals — 0/10
Nothing about this cart makes me feel like a valued customer. There is no loyalty program mention. No "preferred customer" designation. No early access. No limited-edition callouts. No "only 3 remaining" urgency. No personalized greeting. I am a row in a table, indistinguishable from any other customer. The cart treats a $25,000-a-year customer and a first-time browser identically.

### Missing Luxury Features
- Gift wrapping with material/style selection
- Personalized gift notes with typeface preview
- Premium and White Glove shipping tiers
- Estimated delivery date and time windows
- Save for Later / Wishlist integration
- Product recommendations in cart ("Complete the look")
- Concierge chat or personal shopper assistance
- Premium packaging options (gift box, tissue, ribbon)
- Customer loyalty tier recognition
- Order notes for special instructions
- Multiple shipping addresses (for gifts)
- Gift receipts
- Subscription/auto-replenishment option for consumables
- Product care instructions or setup guides
- Carbon-neutral shipping option

## Top 3 Recommendations

**1. Introduce Gift Services — This Is Non-Negotiable**
Add gift wrapping (with visual preview of at least three wrapping options), a personalized note field (with an elegant font preview), and premium packaging as a selectable upgrade. I send cat products as gifts constantly. Every premium retailer I patronize offers this. Its absence is not a missing feature — it is a disqualification from the premium category.

**2. Elevate the Cart's Visual Identity to Match the Products' Ambition**
Replace the HTML table with a card-based layout. Show larger, lifestyle-quality product images. Add brief product descriptions or taglines. Use typography that conveys quality — not the same system font used in every utility application. The cart should feel like a curated collection, not a spreadsheet. If the products are "smart" and "AI-powered," the cart should feel equally intelligent and considered.

**3. Offer Tiered Shipping with White Glove as the Premium Option**
Standard. Priority (2-day). White Glove (scheduled delivery, premium protective packaging, signature required). The flat $10 shipping with no options tells me nothing about when my order arrives, how it will be protected, or whether anyone will be there to receive it. For customers purchasing $150+ items for their beloved companions, shipping is not an afterthought — it is the last mile of the brand experience.

---

## My One Ask

If I could change one thing — just one — it would be this: **give the cart a soul.**

Right now, this cart is a calculator with a coupon field. It knows what I'm buying and how much it costs, and that is the extent of its understanding. It doesn't know that I'm buying the SleepNest ThermoPod because Lady Grey has been sleeping poorly since we replaced the living room furniture. It doesn't know that the PawTrack Smart Collar is a birthday gift for Margaret's Bengal. It doesn't know that I've been a customer for three years and have spent thousands.

A cart with a soul would know these things — or at least create the *space* for them. A note field. A gift option. A personal greeting. A recommendation that says "we understand your cats." Premium brands don't just sell products — they create relationships. And relationships require more than a spreadsheet.

I want to love this platform. The products are genuinely interesting — AI-powered, thoughtfully conceived, priced at a level that suggests quality. But the shopping experience betrays the product promise. Fix the cart, and you begin to earn the kind of customer loyalty that money cannot buy but premium experiences reliably create.
